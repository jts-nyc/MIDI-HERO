# Short student audio trial

September 27, 2026. **Proposed formative protocol; no student results collected.** Independent of MIDI-HERO's current build. This responds to the need to try the experience with students before the user can annotate Guitar Hero audio in a couple of weeks.

## Decision this trial can inform

Can a student hear their assigned keyboard part, tell when it changes, and find the next entrance after an error? The starting hypothesis is **actual played pitches plus steady accompaniment**. The [Guitar Hero pilot](audio-pilot-findings.md) has not validated that policy. This trial can reveal obvious comprehension and recovery problems; it cannot establish durable learning, class-wide effectiveness or original-game mechanics.

## Prepare one small musical task

- Use the original four-bar “First Lights” anchor version in the [arrangement memo](arrangements-evidence.md): C4/G4, C4/E4, C4/G4, E4/C4, with two half notes per bar. It uses eight attacks and three pitches within the initial two-octave range.
- Accompaniment supplies pulse, bass and restrained harmony. Leave the assigned foreground notes to the student. Keep the arrangement and tempo constant when comparing conditions; 100 BPM is the illustration's suggestion, not a required beginner tempo.
- Sound the key actually played, including a wrong pitch. Keep backing steady after mistakes for this first trial. A short count-in and repeatable four-bar passage are proposed trial conveniences.
- Use one audible instrument route: the keyboard's own sound or a software instrument. Check for double monitoring and noticeable response delay before students begin. Record the device, sound route and settings. If those cannot be made reliable, resolve that setup issue before interpreting performance.
- Keep targets and judgment stable. Use whatever standalone presentation supports these requirements; this document does not certify that the existing application supports them.

## About ten minutes per student

1. **Orient:** let the student hear/play each assigned pitch and try the phrase. Ask what they think their part contributes. Record prior keyboard/rhythm-game familiarity briefly.
2. **Check audibility:** demonstrate two passes in counterbalanced order: the assigned part played correctly with backing, and the same backing with one assigned note omitted. Make this probe audio-only: keep the instructor's hands and visual targets out of the student's view, and record that condition. Ask what changed before disclosing the omission. Keep playback gain fixed. These are instructor-controlled probes, not Guitar Hero evidence.
3. **Play:** allow two or three short attempts with actual-note sound and backing. Observe naturally occurring wrong notes, omissions and the next successful entrance. Do not require a student to fail to create data. If no error occurs, mark recovery as not observed.
4. **Ask:** “What did you hear yourself adding?” “Was there a moment that sounded different from what you meant?” “How did you find where to come back in?” Record uncertainty and their words before interpreting them.
5. **Offer a choice:** another try, a simpler/slower practice pass, or stop. Record the choice without prompting for the desired answer. A slower run is practice, not evidence of improvement at the original tempo.
6. **Brief replay:** if time permits, replay once with less visual guidance. Record which assigned notes and entrances remain. This is immediate recall; delayed retention needs a later session.

Start with a few students with varied experience, including those who struggle or stop. Report individual observations and counts with denominators; do not turn a convenience sample into a population estimate.

## One record per student

| Field | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Session | Participant code, experience, device/sound route, arrangement, tempo and settings |
| Audibility probe | Audio-only condition, order, response in their words, omitted note noticed / not noticed / unsure |
| Playing | Attempts, wrong pitch / omitted target / early release as separately observed, or unknown |
| Recovery | Error and next coherent entrance; opportunities passed, with uncertainty; not observed if no error |
| Understanding | Can describe own part and a specific mismatch, with their actual explanation |
| Choice | Voluntary retry / slower practice / stop; stated reason if offered |
| Replay | Same-settings change and reduced-guidance performance, reported separately |
| Evidence | **Heard:** identified listener's report. **Measured:** actual counted/logged result and method. **Documented:** protocol/settings. **Inferred:** investigator interpretation and alternatives. |

Do not call ordinary teacher observation a precise timing measurement. Do not call stated enjoyment a voluntary retry, or a retry proof of learning.

## Decide what to change next

- If the assigned part is hard to hear, adjust arrangement or balance and repeat the audibility probe before adding error effects.
- If wrong playing sounds convincingly correct, check for a doubled automatic lead or assistance that conceals the student action.
- If the beat is audible but students cannot re-enter, investigate phrase lead-in, target clarity and task difficulty; sound alone may not explain the problem.
- If mistakes are audible yet their meaning remains unclear, a later comparison can add one restrained cue while holding everything else fixed. Do not change cue, accompaniment, tempo and judgment together.
- Preserve reports from students who stop or struggle. Summarize what was heard, counted and still ambiguous before selecting the next experiment.

Actual-note sound with/without a cue and performance-dependent accompaniment remain the larger comparisons in the [audio plan](audio-assessment-plan.md). This smaller first session answers whether the basic contribution is intelligible enough to justify those comparisons. Student results and Guitar Hero findings must keep separate sources and conclusions.
